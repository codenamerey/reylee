#### Notes before proceeding
- In Plato's Nicomachean ethics, a virtue is something a person has to live by in order to lead a eudaimonic (happy) life. It is like a knob; it's a spectrum. Tilt the knob way to the left and you end up with a *lack* of a virtue; tilt it way to the right and you end up with *too much* of a virtue.
    - A person must find the balance, what is called the *golden mean*, to properly follow a virtue.

### Generosity: Love and wishes
In Plato's Nicomachean ethics, generosity is a virtue. Give too little of it and you become stingy; give too much and you become extravagant.

What struck me so hard is the idea that generosity is not *giving*, but rather, *sharing*. What you can be generous about is something you already have; something you can share. If you don't have something you want to be generous about, that something, then, becomes a wish.

Romantic love is a great example (and is also what my professor used to illustrate generosity.) It's a popular idea too: only love once you can. It's a cliché, but you can't love another greater than you do yourself.

If you don't already love yourself, you do not have what it takes to be generous about love. You can only treat the other person right as you do yourself. 

The same goes to being a parent, being a friend, being a fellow human. The bottleneck will always be you and how you are to yourself. It's a pretty well established idea dating back to Plato's time way before telenovelas capitalized on it.

### What to have a friend means
Kant puts an extreme ideal on what it means to be friends. Phenomenal thinker, Kant was, and he did all that thinking in his hometown where he spent his entire life in.


Kant acknowledges three other classes of friends, friends of need—one that is a friend due to convenience or advantage, and friends of taste—one that is a friend due to similar interests.

The true friend is the friend of disposition—one Kant says we should strive to be and to have. A friend of disposition never speaks ill of you and one you can lay your whole being to and trust that they will treat it with care.

He said true friendship is where both parties entrust themselves fully in the other without worry that they will hurt you, because you know that in their heart of hearts, they want what is best for you.

The love a true friend has for their other friend is unconditional. A friend does their best to support their friend, and this is mutual.
