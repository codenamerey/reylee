Thinking of having a child? If you think it's going to be easy, think again. I'm sure everyone's being told that parenthood is hard. So it's all the more suprising that a lot of people dive into it without so much of a ponder. If you are thinking of becoming a parent, it is a life-changing decision. If it is for better or for worse will be decided by how you handle parenthood.

I am a 23-year-old childless Gen Z, and if you think that doesn't give me a license to give my thoughts on parenthood, think again. Most parents don't know half the shit they are doing, so how different are they from me? 

They don't have a clue on what they are doing, understandably so, since it is their first time being one, and even if they have multiple attempts on parenthood (i.e., they have lots of children), well, it's never enough. It's a continuous learning experience anyway (although you could choose to never learn, if so, good job).

You will never be enough as a parent; the earlier you accept that, the better. You will traumatize your child one way or the other. Maybe you forget to fetch them on their first day on kindergarten, or you inadvertently pass your own unresolved trauma on them. Hey, nobody's perfect, right? The goal is to minimize it to the best possible minimization. This list attempts on guiding you on just that.

### 2 categories
This list of tips has two categories:
1. Ex post facto --- for when a tip is useful after the fact of offspring-bearing, and;
2. Prospective --- the opposite. Useful if you are pondering on having, or expecting, a child.

An entry has a tag of either of these categories.

### Be a fur parent first (Prospective)
Pets are like children, except they never turn into snobbish teenagers (unless you have cats). It's a good practice to parenthood. If you can attend to a pet's needs: groom them, take them to the vet, walk them outside, know when to indulge them or put a foot forth if they are being difficult without resorting to violence or screaming, then it is a good indicator that you will become a good human parent. You have to hone this patience, because raising a human child is likely a thousand times more demanding and stressful.

If you wake up one day and be greeted by an absolute mess in your living room because Spotty took a potty on where he shouldn't be, and you don't feel the urge to obliterate the little shit, then you'll know that you are one step closer to being a proper parent. How about that?

### Develop yourself in all areas (Prospective)
In Aristotle's Nicomachean ethics, generosity is defined as something that you *share* with, not something you just give. If you aren't capable of something, you can not expect yourself to teach that something to your kid.

Parenthood is also about nourishing your young. It means it is your responsibility to help them achieve their optimal self. If you are not solid on a lot of areas in your life, how can you be their guide on navigating this tumultuous experience we call life? You should at least be confident that you are on your way (i.e., it is clear to you the space you ought to take in the world) to being your best self yourself.

### Read parenting books (Prospective)
Yeah, no shit. Given, theory isn't everything, but don't we send engineers, those that we entrust on building skyscrapers and innovating and finding solutions to technical problems to universities where they are fed theory?

You are told this lie: that parenting is going to be something visceral;  something that becomes obvious as you go on with it. To that, I say, crap! While it is true that there is some maternal or paternal instict awakening after childbirth, that doesn't carry over into other age levels. To be a parent is to be an eternal learner

It is my personal position that it should be mandatory for parents-to-be to read at least a book or two before getting into it. And then a book for dealing with angsty teenagers, and then some more and then some more.

Aside from parenting books, a parent could ask another parent, presumably with more seasoned experience, about stuff. Reliance on those however should be subject to scrutiny.

### Parenthood doesn't make you special
If I had a nickel for every time parenthood is sensationalized, I'd accumulate a hefty sum! Becoming a parent doesn't make you any better or worse than the next person. More so, becoming a parent doesn't make you above your child! In the Philippines, the phrase "anak ka lang" ("you are only my child") is common, and its as meaningless as it is stupid. Really, what does that mean? You're both humans. You might purport, "But I am older!", then I'd like to refer you to my client, Bob, the 60-year old registered sex offender down our block. You could be old having not learned anything at all. You could be young and learned.

Going back on topic, take a moment to self-reflect on how you see, or you'll see, a child, because you might make yourself believe otherwise than what you claim you do. Do you feel above them? Do you think that being a parent of someone somehow makes you superior to them, and that gives you authority to supersede their own agency? You should destroy any similar train of thought.

You should abolish such belief. It doesn't mean if you are the parent that you automatically know better than your child. You should pray, actually, that your kid turns out to be better than you are. Have humility. Acknowledge criticism. Work to be better.

### Your kid is their own person

Think of the most apalling *bias* you may have. What if they turn out to be that way? Ask yourself, for example: what if they turn out to be gay? What would your reaction be in that scenario?

Accept that they might be different from you. A child is yet another human being. Yes, earlier in their life, they'll need most of your guidance; they'll do the same things you do, believe the same things, act the same way. I know, it's cute.

But kids aren't kids forever. They go on to the world and become their own person. You'll have conflicts with them in a lot of areas. You'll fail to recognize the person you raised. You might have fundamental differences.

You don't own your kid. You don't get to decide who the friends they want to make are, or whether or not they'll smoke weed, do drugs. It is life's reality!  

While you can steer them to be in some way, you don't have control over everything, and you shouldn't expect them to be your replica; they might deviate, slightly or dramatically.  Deal with it.

When a kid becomes an adult, they are literally legally emancipated. You must endeavor to be their light, yes, but that is all you will be: a guide. You do not get to decide the person your kid will become, neither should you want to decide for them. It is, actually, good practice early on to teach them not only independence, but individuality. Let them have a say on what wallpaper goes on the wall, ask their perspective on some decisions, encourage them to expand their horizons and to explore the world out there. Teach them that it is a world of possibilities out there. Let them know that they can have different opinions than you do, and that you'll love them regardless.

### Finishing Point: What is your rationale?
Ask yourself, "why do I want to have kids?". If the answer to the question is "oh, I want to have mini-mes! It'd be so cute" or "this kid will be my redemption" or "This kid is the answer to my poverty" or "this kid will save our marriage", do the world a favor and don't.

If the center of the nature of the want is you, don't proceed on childbearing. Parenthood should be less about you and more on the humans you will bear without their consent.
