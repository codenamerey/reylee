### AI Mustn't replace humans
#### Musings on AI development, human dignity, and the future of work

I am scared about losing my software engineering job due to AI, especially as my chosen vocation is what the leading LLM AI providers are benchmarking their latest models against.

I used to not just be scared, but be scared *shitless*. You see memes going around and fearmongering articles about the death of software engineering as a profession going around. I remember the first time I watched AI write a website right before me. I was using Claude, before Claude Code. I asked it to write a calculator, which it heeded, and there were a bugs here and there, but I remember just sitting there in awe, and in angst. This was in 2025, and AI was just getting better—I knew that, everyone knew that. I got this existential dread about the security of the career I seemed to have planned all of my 20s about.

I shared with my senior engineers about this fear and have often asked their guidance on the matter. Actually, I asked anyone who is in the industry who'd listen at my frantic dreadings. None were sure.

Of course. Who's actually to know how good AI will be at doing human desk jobs? Right now, their reliability in programming is a flip of a coin. They are better in other knowledge work, and who is to say how much these models are going to improve in the next year? the next 5?

Governments are catching up, passing bills on barring abrupt company terminations of their employees. This is a real, acknowledged fear—that a significant number of people will lose their purchasing power.

Whether AI will reach a point where it will be able to replace humans at most jobs, this article doesn't explore. The question of whether we should let AI take them over, however, is what it does: we mustn't.

We should establish what we want AI for. It should be not to be good enough to replace humans, but to enhance humans.

I am working on my AI-and-human-in-the-loop learning platform project with this in mind. If AI is superhuman, then we must use that leverage to make humans, super.

What is bothering to me was that the expectation of the ever-improving AI models is it's going to make a lot of people obsolete. I can imagine nobody wants that to happen economically speaking.

Elon Musk quipped that an upside to the job loss is that, eventually, resources will be abundant nobody will need money anymore. Even, suppose, an unlikely happy ending is to happen, what'll we do? Sit in front of the television screen eating salami off our chests 'til the end of time?

What is the ultimate goal of everything?
